E-commerce seller compliance

Marketplace settlement reports rarely agree with your books on the first pass. Reconciling them before filing is what keeps the notices away.

Get a quote Ask on WhatsApp

Selling on a marketplace produces three parallel records: your invoices, the platform's settlement report, and the TCS collected and reported by the operator under section 52. The GST return has to agree with all three, and returns, commission, shipping deductions and cancelled orders are where they diverge.

The second problem is geography. Stock held at a marketplace fulfilment centre in another state usually means a place of business in that state — and therefore a registration, and a return cycle, in each one.

What we handle

  • Reconciliation of marketplace settlement reports with your sales register and books
  • TCS credit under section 52 claimed and matched on the portal
  • Multi-state GST registration where stock is held at fulfilment centres
  • Treatment of returns, cancellations, commission, shipping and platform fees
  • GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B filing for each registration on the monthly cycle
  • Input credit on commission and logistics invoices raised by the platform
  • Place of supply determination for inter-state orders
  • Income tax return and books for the selling entity, including presumptive options where eligible
  • TDS credit under section 194-O reconciled against 26AS
  • Notices arising from a difference between reported turnover and marketplace data

What you supply

  • Settlement or payment reports from each marketplace for the period
  • Order and returns reports, in the platform's export format
  • Commission and fee invoices raised by the platform
  • Bank statements showing settlements received
  • List of fulfilment centres where your stock is held
  • Existing GST registrations across states

Export the reports rather than screenshotting the dashboard. Reconciliation depends on order-level data that the summary view does not carry.

Frequently missed

Stock in another state's warehouse

A seller who has never left their home state can still have a place of business in four others, because their inventory sits there. This is one of the most common causes of a registration notice for online sellers.

Questions we are asked about this

I only sell online and my turnover is small. Do I still need GST registration?

Supply through an e-commerce operator generally requires registration regardless of turnover, with narrow exceptions. Most marketplaces will not let you list without a GSTIN in any case.

The marketplace already collects TCS. Do I still file returns?

Yes. TCS collected by the operator is a credit in your electronic cash ledger, not a substitute for your own return. It has to be claimed and matched on the portal.

Why does my reported turnover not match the platform's figure?

Usually returns, cancellations and the treatment of commission and shipping. The platform reports gross order value while your books carry net sales. The difference has to be explainable, which is what the reconciliation produces.

I sell on three platforms. Is that three sets of everything?

Three sets of reconciliation, but one set of returns per GST registration. We consolidate across platforms before filing so nothing is reported twice.

Send one month of settlement reports

One month is usually enough to show whether your returns and your marketplace data agree — and where they do not.
